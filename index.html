After a Major Change, Sweep These

A rename, a moved tool, or a rephrasing ripples further than the screen it happens on. This is the sweep list — each entry is a place stale wording has actually been found.

Help dots
Every “?” whose screen changed. A rebuilt screen can orphan its help dot entirely — check the dot still exists, and that its text describes the screen as it now stands.
Explainers behind cones
Planned-tool text that names other tools, desks, or old vocabulary — including the footer state when a cone comes off.
Plain Words
If the market’s vocabulary moved, the ledger records it — old word kept in quotation marks as the bridge.
App Glossary
If the app’s own vocabulary moved — a desk renamed, a new kind of part invented — the glossary follows.
Crumbs and desk homes
A moved tool must crumb back to its new desk, and nothing should still say where it used to live.
Cross-references in other tools’ text
Summaries, alerts, tile descriptions and Records Needed entries that name the changed thing by its old name.
The handoff document
The next session inherits whatever the handoff says — it must describe the app as shipped, not as remembered.

The market’s words live in Plain Words. These are the app’s own words — the vocabulary this project uses for its parts, so a conversation about the app can be exact.

What This Screen Cannot Change

This screen changes numbers and dates. It cannot change how the market works.

Adding a booth request to Sunday, reordering who chooses first in a Booth Selection, adding a new approval step, changing who a rule applies to, or adding a screen — all of that is a change to the app itself and needs whoever maintains it. The same goes for anything not listed above: if a number is not on this screen, it is still written into the code.

Two things this screen does not do on its own. It does not rewrite a season that has already closed — a fee changed today does not restate last year’s invoices. And on a device with no shared storage, a change made here stays on that device.

New licences, permits and certificates do not belong here at all: add those in Vendor Requirements, which was built for exactly that.

What This Screen Can and Cannot Do

Committee membership is subject to Board approval, and chairs are appointed by the Board President (Bylaws, Article VI). This screen records those decisions so everyone can see them — it does not make them. Add people here after the Board has said yes, not before.

Time served on a committee counts toward a vendor’s work hour requirement. Hours still get logged the normal way, through Work Hours — the committee chair keeps the meeting tally.
